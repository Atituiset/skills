# Motion direction

## Turn an explanation into an action

Before choosing an effect, answer: what enters the scene, what operation changes it, and what observable result proves the point? Write those three facts in the storyboard. Examples:

| Concept | Actor and action | Visible result |
|---|---|---|
| Recurrent state | A token updates successive hidden states; trace the path of an earlier clue | The path from an early clue to a later output becomes longer |
| Attention | A chain opens into a network; a query reads selected positions | A distant relevant token becomes directly accessible |
| Sparse experts | Inputs arrive at a router and activate selected branches | Most experts remain inactive for that step |
| State update | Incoming information changes a persistent state | History influences output without drawing every pair |
| KV Cache | Existing K/V remain in place while a new position is written | History is reused rather than recomputed |
| Speculative decoding | Draft tokens advance together and are accepted/corrected | Several proposed positions share a verification step |

Label simplified examples as illustrations where counts or weights could be mistaken for measurements.

## Choose a coherent direction, not a fixed skin

Possible directions include a precise light editorial diagram, an instrument-like dark spatial environment, tactile paper geometry, a restrained code laboratory, or an organic network. Pick one from the topic and user preference; combine only compatible elements. A dark background, glow and floating dots are supporting treatments, not the story.

Use motion hierarchy:

1. The primary action explains the mechanism.
2. A reveal or camera move directs attention to its consequence.
3. Ambient motion supports continuity without competing with the narration.

A quiet explanation can hold. More dynamic does not mean every object must move continuously. Save contrast and motion amplitude for the point that matters.

## Continuity

Track a persistent actor, coordinate system or object identity between scenes. Transform an established diagram where that makes the causal relationship clearer. A layout that dissolves every few seconds still feels like slides even with decorative animation.

Plan the last readable state of scene A and the first readable state of scene B together. Motion can carry a shared token, trace an existing connection, fold a matrix, or reveal a larger structure. Keep labels attached to their objects and establish the next stage promptly.

Narration cues control the mechanism's reveal. Camera drift may span the scene, but it must not expose a solution before the explanation or crop a premise while it is still being discussed.

## Implement and inspect

Use `useCurrentFrame()` and deterministic interpolation/springs. Time, randomness and CSS animations independent of the Remotion frame cannot reliably reproduce a seeked frame. Store seeded variation if it is useful.

SVG is effective for paths, graph structure and token motion; perspective layers can establish depth without a full 3D scene. Use true 3D when spatial relationships earn its rendering cost. Render paths beneath their nodes, terminate connections outside labels, and inspect transformed extents at the largest zoom and pan.

For “more visual / more dynamic”, compare the before/after playback. At least one mechanism should visibly develop through an action, not merely fade in with a new palette. Validate a complete action including its setup and consequence, not only its most attractive still.

## Mechanism and research-history films

Carry one concrete input through successive mechanisms. Neural nodes must have a defined meaning: neuron, vector position, hidden state or module; do not conflate them. Show information moving, which connections change, and the result. Decorative firing alone does not explain computation. Anchor paper names and dates to structural changes, distinguish preprint from publication dates, and use branches for parallel research rather than a false replacement ladder. Use a compact paper → bottleneck → mechanism change → tradeoff mapping to choose scenes; display the relevant title and year when the structural change occurs. Select the papers needed for the narrative rather than reproducing every timeline entry.
